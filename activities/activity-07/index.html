Overview

North Shore Oʻahu is known for wide sandy beaches, clear water, and big winter waves. Some spots are calm and perfect for swimming in the summer, while others are famous for surfing when the swell is up. This page is a simple guide to a few popular beaches and what makes each one different.

Sunset Beach

Sunset Beach is long, open, and known for sunsets that look unreal when the sky is clear. In winter, this area is famous for large surf and surf competitions. In calmer seasons, people walk the shoreline, watch the ocean, and enjoy the wide space.

Banzai Pipeline (Ehukai)

Banzai Pipeline is one of the most well-known surf breaks in the world. The waves break over a shallow reef, which creates thick, fast barrels. Even if you don’t surf, it’s a popular place to watch expert surfers from the sand when conditions are right.

Pūpūkea / Shark’s Cove

Shark’s Cove is part of Pūpūkea Beach Park and is well known for snorkeling when the ocean is calm. In summer, the tide pools and rocky areas can be full of fish and clear water views. In winter, the surf can crash into the rocks, so it’s more of a look-and-leave-it spot during rough conditions.

Waimea Bay

Waimea Bay is a classic North Shore beach with a big open shoreline and a deep blue bay. In the summer, the water is often calm and people swim, snorkel, and jump from the rock near the edge of the bay. In the winter, the waves can get powerful and the beach becomes a major surf spot.

Safety Note

Ocean conditions on the North Shore can change quickly. Always check the surf and weather before getting in the water, and pay attention to posted signs. If the waves look large or the current looks strong, it’s okay to enjoy the view from shore.
